# Exam planning and review

Before making a plan, guide, quiz, map, or case: read the exam README, Priority Map, applicable unit README, and cited official scope/objectives.

## Build the priority map

For each objective, record: source/location; included topics; the action the student must demonstrate; related units; current evidence of ability; next practice task. Use the exam template.

Separate must-know objectives from supporting detail. Use official weights only if supplied. Otherwise label suggested priorities as study recommendations, not predicted exam percentages. Identify scope conflicts.

Choose priorities by objective importance, prerequisite gaps, observed weakness, and remaining confirmed time. Never invent an exam date, question count, format, or instructor preference.

## Exam review

Organize as applicable:

- Must know and high yield, tied to objectives.
- Commonly confused concepts and compare/contrast.
- Methods, formulas, evidence, vocabulary, or labs relevant to the subject.
- Priority/safety for disciplines where it matters.
- Treatments/medications only when in scope.
- Applications, complications/limitations, and evaluation.
- Practice questions with answers withheld when interactive.

Do not duplicate entire lectures. Link to existing source-backed notes and build short targeted summaries. Supporting textbook detail must be labeled.

## Study plan

Propose achievable sessions around the student's available time. Mix retrieval practice, weak areas, spaced revisits, and exam-style application. Record a plan as proposed until the student adopts it. Do not schedule external reminders without their request.

After an exam, record results only when the student supplies them. Use actual feedback to update course progress; preserve useful cumulative-review topics without carrying all old files into every new chat.
